2 · EDA — data & fields

What one row means, and which fields to trust

One row of Dataset.parquet is one order line: an item in one size in one order. 15,179,907 lines, 8,627,791 orders, 1,500 style-colours, 6,979 SKUs, 1 Jan 2024 to 30 Jun 2026 with no missing days. Hover a row for how the field is used.

FieldTypeProfile

The target, exactly

Sum of qty_ordered on lines with cancelled = FALSE, grouped by the calendar month of ny_date, all classes (ONLINE, RETAIL, DROP) and all channels, per style_color. Quantities are multiplied by a constant for confidentiality; forecast in the same scaled units.

99.4% of lines have qty 1; the largest line is 62. Units on non-cancelled lines: 14,984,773.

How the raw file was tamed

The machine has about 0.5 GB of free memory, so the 249 MB parquet is never loaded into pandas. eda/02_build_aggs.py uses DuckDB to write four small aggregates, and every later script works from those.

  • data/daily_item_chan.parquet: item × day × class × channel × cancelled, 1,616,758 rows, with lines, orders, unit-weighted discount and size counts.
  • data/items.parquet: one row per item, with consistency counts (names, types, collections) and first and last sale.
  • data/daily_otype.parquet: day × class × raw channel tag; used for store openings.
  • data/monthly_item_size.parquet: item × month × size, non-cancelled.

Second source: item_attributes.csv

1,329 of the 1,500 items (96 of the 100 targets) carry visual-tagger attributes. Values are free text with many spellings of the same thing. Fabric weight alone has 36 spellings ("Medium", "Midweight", "Medium Weight", "Regular"…), so the weight was collapsed to light / medium / heavy. For the other attributes, the top categories were kept, the rest became "other" and missing values "untagged". Missing shares: secondary colour 71%, rise 65%, neckline 35%, sleeve 35%. In the backtests the attributes added nothing measurable once an item has sales.

5 · EDA — seasonality

Each product type has its own year

2025 monthly index per product type: 1.0 is that type's monthly average. The outlined columns are the forecast months. These come from raw type totals, so they include the item-mix effect; read the shape, not the level. Hover a cell for the type's volume and how many targets it holds.

Occasion tags, 2025 index

Merchandising tags carried on every line. 39 of the 100 targets are tagged. World Cup items (20, all launched in late May or early June 2026) have no history.

What this means for July to October

  • The window opens on the back-to-school peak (August about 1.2× the monthly average for untagged items) and steps down to about 0.8 in September and October.
  • Hoodies, sweaters, sweatpants, jackets and cardigans are at their trough in June and July (0.2–0.5) and climb to 0.8–1.0 by October.
  • Short jeans, skirts and skorts peak in July and August (1.5–2.1) and fall back by October; swimwear falls from July.
  • Halloween items peak in October (1.78), Homecoming in September (2.25); 4th of July items fade from September.
  • November and December are 2–3× normal for most types (Black Friday). They sit outside the window, but they distort the recent level at winter origins.

7 · EDA — items & sizes

Who the items are

Items by year published

Other itemsThe 100 targets

Demand is concentrated: the top 10% of items sell 53% of units; the 100 targets sell 21%. Not intermittent: only 0.8% of item-months after launch are zero and 2.8% are under 10 units. The median item-month sells 206 units.

Size mix by category group

Size mix is stable, so size-level stock-outs are easy to spot: a core size selling nothing while the others sell. That happens in only 0.5% of item-weeks and hides about 0.1% of online units.

Colour extensions

color_num 1 is the original colour; 2+ are colours added later. 33 targets are extensions, and 16 targets share a style with another target.

Item-master quirks

  • published_date is stored at midnight Israel time, so it falls on the previous day in New York. Convert it before computing age.
  • 85 items sell before their published date (up to 176 days). The effective start is the earlier of published date and first sale.
  • 190 items were published before 2024, so their early history is unobserved. Age uses the true start; exposure uses the start clipped to 1 Jan 2024.
  • An item can carry up to 3 product names, 3 product types and 2 collections across its lines. Use the most recent or most common value.
  • Collections (C2023-05-LA) encode launch batch: year, month and origin.
8 · EDA — artefacts

Fifteen things in the data that will bite you

The brief asks for these explicitly. Hover a row for the evidence. Treatments are applied in the scripts named.

#ArtefactTreatmentWhere

Stock-outs without inventory data

Item level: a zero run is flagged when it is improbable at the item's own rate (Poisson, P < 0.1%, at least 3 days). That gives 1,716 runs on 664 items, 2.7% of live item-days but only about 0.4% of units. 47 runs reach the end of the data.

Size level: a core size (≥ 15% share) sells nothing in a week where the other sizes imply ≥ 7 units: 516 of 95,406 item-weeks, about 0.1% of online units.

Decision: no global de-censoring (D021). Two targets are handled individually: S16079_DARK-GRAY, with no sales since 10 April (forecast as in stock, D034), and S14995_NAVY, which lost about 15% of recent online units to a missing size (D038).

The October 2025 cancellation event

On 14 Oct 2025, 145,109 units were cancelled (74% of the day), almost all on edikted.com; 15 October was 25%. Every other month sits near 1%. Cancelled lines are excluded from the target, as the brief says.

10 · Modelling

From one quantile model to a stack of five

Every idea, including those from the M5 and broader competition reviews, was tested on the same 13 rolling origins (end of Feb 2025 to end of Feb 2026). Each origin forecasts the next four months using only data available at the time. Scoring is on the four-month total per item, the unit the confidence scale uses. Since D109 every system is scored as a ratio to R3 (each item's last 3 months × 4), and small differences are judged by a paired item bootstrap.

The submission (nx13, 18 Nov): six models with a launch-weighted tree for new items, corrections that include look-alikes, a campaign uplift for the plan's two new sales, a confidence rule checked against the brief's scale, and a Model explanation written coarse to fine (summary, models, information, all the main metrics, the cascade as explainability, improvement roadmap), the buying simulation of the submitted system itself and a metrics-versus-baselines sheet

  • System: nx5 of D361 (rules, Huber tree, NN, both TabPFN members, the per-location recommender at a quarter weight) where items under six months old get a Huber tree trained with new items' rows counted five times (D389h), the corrections refitted on that system, and ranges recalibrated per age and volume group with last July–October and the latest March–June weighted four times.
  • Backtest, WAPE ÷ R3 (nx4 in brackets): recent 0.3806 (0.3851), held out 0.2926 (0.2948), all 13 0.4357 (0.4399), early 0.6593 (0.6623). Level with D361's nx5 (recent 0.3811, held out 0.2924, all 13 0.4358).
  • The new-item lift: the 24 targets under six months rise 10.6% (194,622 → 215,244). In the backtest routing moved new items by only 0.3% while the system under-forecast them by 7–12%: the direction holds, the size is new. Stated in the workbook's "Where it is weak".
  • Workbook: 16 explanations in plain words (what drove the number, what the models said, not adjusted); the Model explanation opens with the 15 data artefacts and their treatment.
  • Overnight after it (D389j–o): separate tuning of the new-item and old-item trees, a grid of simple versions, full-strength corrections, per-item weights, two-window calibration: none passes the held-out test and the gate. The monthly split was scored for the first time and kept. The one change that held is the confidence column (D389k): candidate_nx6 (D389n) puts level 3 only where the calibrated range gives less than even odds of landing within ±50% (claims met on the held-out origins 0.75 → 0.81); levels 10 · 86 · 4. Submitted as nx6 on the user's word (D389t).
  • Campaign plan (D389v–x): four of the six planned sales recur on the same dates as 2024 and 2025 and are learnt from last year; the two new ones (Clearance 12–19 Aug, 24–29 Sep) are added explicitly: August and September × exp(0.22 × new sale days ÷ month days × the item's edikted.com share), half the measured ×1.55 campaign-day lift. Backtested: recent 0.3806 → 0.3796, held out 0.2926 → 0.2916, Mar–Jun 26 0.2465 → 0.2447 (early origins worse). +8,959 units. As tree inputs the calendar lost in three forms (D389w).

The submitted system (D274)

  • Anchor: R3, then the product type's like-for-like seasonality.
  • Five members, each forecasting the 4-month total: the rule agents; a quantile LightGBM with events, co-purchase neighbours and launch curves; a sales-curve neural net; TabPFN v2 with 32 estimators; TabPFN v2 with conformally calibrated quantiles.
  • Stack: the arithmetic mean of the five, in units. The geometric mean forecast low and bought worse (D230).
  • Corrections: a segment re-allocation (volume × age × discount) and a breakout model, combined by geometric mean with each origin's total kept (D245, D247, D259).
  • Months: the total is split with the rule agent's and the NN's monthly profiles (D121).
  • Buying quantity: the same stack with the quantile members at P65 (D236).
  • Confidence: the conformal TabPFN member's P25–P75 width, one level worse where the members differ by more than 2× (D237).

What changed after the first submission

The 12 Nov submission was the quantile model below plus judgement rules (580,968 units). Each round was tested on the same 13 origins; decision ids open the log.

  • Rule agents, NN and the three-way stack. Explicit rules on the recent rate, a neural net with hierarchical embeddings and a sales-curve encoder, combined with the quantile model: 0.440 × R3. A lean feature set, occasion timing and a Black Friday fix followed.
  • Weather and events. Statewide temperature and a US events calendar, kept alongside seasonality: three-way 0.4234; with the Black Friday fix 0.4222 and 11.05% buying loss. Replacing seasonality outright was worse.
  • Carts, style embeddings and the cart gate. Co-purchase neighbours and LLM fashion scores helped (0.4326). Rule from D160: an embedding must predict cart co-occurrence better than raw product features before it is used. Content embeddings v4 and v6 (with photos) pass; a graph network is level with them; as tree features they add nothing. A Poisson co-purchase network re-fitted at every origin does help: 0.4202.
  • Explainability. SHAP of each item's deviation from R3, grouped into 11 categories by an LLM and scored by three LLM judges; simple surrogates on the categories recover 72–85% of the lift. For the submitted stack the categories explain 94% of the deviation.
  • Foundation models. Time-series models (Chronos-2, Bolt, TimesFM) rejected at 0.63–0.76 alone. TabDPT, Mitra, Nori and TabICL rejected or conditional. TabPFN v2 adopted as a member: 0.4100 recent, 10.78% buying loss.
  • The M5 round. Rejected: hierarchical reconciliation, campaign-calendar features, learned combination weights, Google Trends, multi-horizon neural nets, augmentation, weekly origins, other boosting libraries, tuning, bagging, cluster and trend features. Adopted: conformal TabPFN quantiles, the arithmetic stack, and the P65 buying column (buying loss follows bias, not WAPE).
  • Decomposition and errors. Looks hold twice the shared error of product types, but no group-level model beats the sum of items. The error is concentrated in young launches that break out into the peak. Two corrections with totals kept moved the system to 0.3902.
  • Fixable errors. The tree member had no age feature. Adding 15 event windows and launch curves and dropping the static descriptors: 0.3961 before corrections, 0.3871 after. Leakage and memorisation checks clean; a survivorship limit found. A launch plan at look level would be worth about 1% but needs Edikted's calendar.
  • Rebuilds and switches. TabPFN rebuilt on the new features is better alone but level in the stack; the NN rebuilt on them is better: 0.3851 recent, 0.4399 on all 13. The submission was switched to candidate_nx and then to candidate_nx2. On 17 November the World Cup hand rule was restored on top (candidate_nx3), then the confidence column was set by a validated rule (within ±20% in the backtest: level 1 58% of the time, level 2 44%, level 3 28%) and the ranges widened upward to 80% coverage, with units unchanged (candidate_nx4).

History: the quantile model behind the 12 Nov submission

  • Rows: one per item × origin. Target: log((units over the next 4 months + 1) / (4 × level + 1)).
  • Quantiles: 10th, 25th, 40th, 50th, 60th, 75th and 90th percentile, each the average of a base-feature and an all-feature LightGBM, sorted to remove crossing.
  • Answer: the median. At the June and July 2025 origins, the ones that match this task, the median has zero bias (the 61st percentile is +9% to +12%). The 61st percentile used before zeroed bias pooled over nine origins, but that bias came from the Black Friday windows (D050).

History: the monthly model of rounds 1–3

  • Rows: item × month-end origin × horizon 1–4 (data/features.parquet, 85,264 rows over 28 origins). An item is eligible once live ≥ 30 days and selling in the last 56.
  • Target: log((units + 1) / (level + 1)), where level = units in the last 91 days ÷ days live in that window × 30.44.
  • Loss: absolute error, so the model predicts a median. This fits non-intermittent demand scored as a percentage error per item.
  • Structure: one pooled LightGBM with the horizon as a feature. Not split by horizon or by category.
  • Ensemble: equal average of a base-feature and an all-feature model.
  • Base features: six monthly lags relative to level, log level, age, product type, target month, horizon, colour number, own prior-year seasonal ratio, occasion flags.
  • All features add: 7- and 28-day pace, 91-day trend, peak ratio, days live, retail / drop / TikTok share, item and relative discount, like-for-like seasonal ratios by type, category group and occasion, and tagger attributes.
  • Hyper-parameters: 300 trees, learning rate 0.05, 15 leaves, min 40 per leaf, 0.8 row and column subsampling, L2 5, seed 0. Not tuned.
  • Final fit: 71,487 rows from 27 origins whose targets are known by June 2026.

How confidence levels are set, and how they score

The assignment's scale: 1 = the 4-month total lands within ±20% of actual, 2 = within ±50%, 3 = off by more. The submitted rule uses the conformal TabPFN member's range around its median, w = (P75 − P25) ÷ (2 × P50):

  • 1 w ≤ 0.2: 20 targets.
  • 2 w ≤ 0.5: 67 targets.
  • 3 wider, or one level worse where the five members differ by more than 2×: 13 targets.

The stated levels rank items correctly but only weakly (quadratic kappa +0.11). The member's P10–P90 range, put on the final forecast, covers 80.6% of actuals (D266, D273).
